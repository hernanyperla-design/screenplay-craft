# Changelog

## 2026-11-11

- Added a rule for which genre section governs when two disagree, with scope notes on
  Sections VII and VIII for commercial thrillers.
- Added candidate guidance to Section IX: plans said aloud, humor as a job, loud set
  pieces inside compact blocks, and fear scaled to the lead's experience.
- Added three candidate revision questions for commercial thrillers: what a win cost a
  careful antagonist, whether a change of course raises the price, and who acts in the
  last set piece.
- Every addition is a candidate drawn from one project. No stable rule changed.

## 2026-11-03

- Clarified when to apply a dialogue-only plot test and how to check a visual discovery.
- Added revision questions for page cuts and broken promises, plus two candidate checks for speculative suspense and survival scenes.

## 2026-09-28

- Added a portable, standard-library source-quotation validator and pre-revision
  receipt check, with synthetic offline regression tests.
- Required exact attributed draft evidence, separate labeling of interpretations,
  and rejection of invalid or stale critiques before automatic revision.
- Moved the shared skill workflow into the repository and made refresh failures
  visible in the new bootstrap. Older skills reading the updated codex receive
  the evidence requirement too.
- Kept private scripts, exemplars, and the local rewrite engine out of the repo.
- These are mechanical safeguards. No new creative rule or claim of improved
  creative quality is promoted by this release.

## 2026-09-08

### Added

- Commercial-thriller two-read test for dialogue orientation and full physical suspense.
- Exposition-under-pressure and measurable-danger principles.
- Guidance that lean writing must preserve consequential decision and reaction time.
- Backstory and trauma test tying past events to present behavior and climax choice.
- Source-format preservation, approval boundaries, revision provenance, native pagination checks, and doctrine-promotion gates.
- A public evaluation standard based on blind preference, held-out projects, and complete mechanical validation.

### Changed

- Qualified blanket anti-exposition language so plot clarity and emotional subtext can coexist.
- Qualified blanket bans on reader-facing and editorial language as register-dependent defaults.

The additions were generalized from repeated revision outcomes across multiple feature projects. No screenplay text, private project canon, or copyrighted corpus material is included.
