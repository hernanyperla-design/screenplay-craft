# When a revision reads faster but feels weaker

Use the question that fits the note. A cut made to an approximate length and a cut made for a fixed slot have different constraints.

## Across genres

**Read across the cut.** Does the next choice still make sense, and do you believe the person would make it now? You may understand why someone could forgive an old friend several lines before you believe they're ready. Listen for the time a laugh, an apology or a scare needs to land. A repeated request can be the moment someone finally agrees to help. A recap can earn its place when the listener draws a new conclusion. With a flexible page target, compare the versions at the moment the extra space buys. With a hard cap, recover the space elsewhere or find a shorter way to carry the turn.

**Trace what one person thinks another has promised.** When a betrayal feels abrupt, go back to the agreement. What did each person hear? What did the promise get someone to do? What does that person notice later that gives them reason to doubt it? A line as ordinary as “I’ll wait outside” can earn a reaction much later when the door opens onto an empty corridor. A deliberate shock can still work if the audience understands its meaning afterward.

**Use the dialogue-only read to answer the writer's brief.** It can expose two voices that sound alike or an objective an audience could never guess. Restore the action, read the exchange again, and see whether a glance, object or refusal already supplies the missing fact. Give someone a line when they need to ask, challenge, conceal or decide. When the writer requires a dialogue-only read to carry the plot, honor that requirement.

## Candidates for speculative suspense and survival stories

**Can someone use the world's rule to learn or do something?** Trace an earlier observable limit, who saw it, and the choice it makes possible under pressure. For example, a recorded voice speaks through a door panel but only a live voice can unlock it. Hearing her brother's voice proves little. Hearing the bolt slide gives the listener a reason to act. If someone else has an override, the listener can suspect her brother is there without having proof. Leave room for discoveries whose purpose is wonder.

**Which danger changes the next move?** In a rescue, identify what will fail first and what someone does because of it. An established longer deadline can wait until it changes a route, costs them an ally or rules out shelter. Keep both clocks in the scene when they force competing choices.

The first three questions put existing revision principles to work. The genre questions are candidates for trying on a relevant scene. Record a counterexample as well as a success before proposing wider use. The [evaluation standard](EVALUATION.md) governs promotion.
