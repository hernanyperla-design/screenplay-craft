# Evaluation Standard

The objective is not to produce prose that merely sounds "screenplay-like." The system should make scene, character, structure, and revision decisions that survive blind comparison with professional work while remaining faithful to the writer's voice.

No model may declare that objective achieved by grading its own output.

## Primary Test

Use blind human preference under matched conditions:

1. Give the current system and candidate system the same scene context, canon, source text, task, model, and output constraints.
2. Remove system labels and randomize presentation order.
3. Let the writer choose A, B, a tie, or neither.
4. Record why the preferred version won.
5. Repeat on held-out scenes and projects that were not used to create the candidate rule.

The writer's blind preference is the primary creative measure. Model graders, edit distance, and style similarity are supporting diagnostics only.

## Creative Axes

Use these axes to explain a result, not to replace the overall human choice:

1. Page-turn pull
2. Character voice and human speech
3. Causality and escalation
4. Exposition delivered under pressure
5. Playable physical specificity
6. Economy without flattened emotion
7. Fidelity to canon, register, and approved scope
8. Ending agency and thematic completion

## Mechanical Gate

A creative improvement fails if it causes any of the following:

- invalid FDX or a broken round trip,
- missing or incorrect revision stars,
- changes outside the approved scope,
- loss of writer-authored text,
- a project-canon contradiction,
- a wrong source-of-truth format, or
- private or copyrighted material entering the portable repository.

Mechanical checks must pass completely.

## Promotion Levels

- **Candidate:** inferred from one result or one project. Record it, but do not apply it broadly.
- **Working draft:** approved by the writer and supported by repeated outcomes. Apply only in its named register while testing continues.
- **Stable:** wins representative blind comparisons, passes the mechanical gate, and does not regress held-out projects or genres.
- **Retired:** loses later testing, conflicts with a stronger rule, or proves too narrow to justify continued use.

## Professional-Parity Claim

Do not claim that the system is indistinguishable from a professional screenwriter until:

1. blind readers cannot reliably distinguish system work from the writer's approved professional-standard work,
2. the result holds across dialogue, action, structure, revision, and endings,
3. it repeats on held-out projects and genres,
4. outside professional readers confirm the result, and
5. the system continues to pass scope, continuity, format, and provenance checks.

The target is consistency, not one exceptional scene.
